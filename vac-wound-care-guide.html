Wound Care
VAC / NPWT Therapy
Vacuum-Assisted Closure — Dressing Guide
Clinical Reference
How VAC Therapy Works Controlled sub-atmospheric pressure removes excess exudate, contracts wound edges (macrostrain), and stimulates granulation tissue through microdeformation of the wound bed.

V.A.C.® Dressing Application — Basic Technique
Watch before working through the steps below.
1
Pre-medicate
Administer prescribed analgesia 30–45 minutes before the dressing change.
2
Cleanse
Irrigate wound bed with sterile saline or ordered solution. Thoroughly dry intact surrounding skin before proceeding.
3
Protect Peri-Wound
Apply skin protectant barrier wipes to healthy skin surrounding the wound to prevent maceration and improve drape adhesion.
Reference Infographic — Assess & Prepare Peri-Wound Skin
1
Size Foam Precisely
Measure wound length, width, and depth. Cut foam — black polyurethane or white polyvinyl alcohol — to fit within wound edges only.
2
Avoid Overlap
Ensure foam does not rest on intact, healthy skin — this causes skin breakdown and undermines therapy.
3
Count & Record Pieces
If using multiple pieces, ensure piece-to-piece contact to transfer pressure. Record the total count on the drape label and in the patient chart.

⚠ Critical Safety Never cut foam directly over the open wound bed. Cut away from the wound, then place.
Reference Infographic — Cut & Fit the Foam Filler
1
Apply Drape
Trim clear adhesive film to cover the foam and extend 3–5 cm onto healthy peri-wound skin. Apply smoothly — no stretching or creases.
2
Create Access Opening
Pinch the drape over the foam centre and cut a 1–2.5 cm hole. Do not cut into the foam itself.
Tip — Smooth First, Cut Second Work from the wound outward when smoothing the drape. Any crease will become a leak pathway under suction.
Reference Infographic — Apply Drape & Cut Access Hole
1
Position Port
Centre the SensaT.R.A.C. pad or suction head directly over the hole cut in the drape.
2
Connect & Initiate
Connect pad tubing to canister tubing. Turn unit on and set negative pressure to the ordered target.
3
Verify Seal
Confirm foam collapses to a firm, raisin-like texture. Listen for whistling leaks — patch any loose edges with additional drape strips.
Target Pressure −75 to −125 mmHg
Seal Verification Checklist
Foam has collapsed to firm, raisin-like texture
No audible whistling or air leak at drape edges
Suction is maintained — unit not alarming
Foam count recorded on drape label and in chart
Tubing unobstructed and secured away from bony prominences
🚫 Contraindications
VAC therapy is contraindicated in the presence of:
  • Non-enteric or unexplored fistulas
  • Necrotic tissue or eschar — must be fully debrided first
  • Untreated osteomyelitis
  • Active malignancy within the wound bed
🩸 Vessel & Organ Protection
Never place foam directly over exposed blood vessels, organs, or nerves.

Place a non-adherent protective layer (e.g. silicone contact layer) over any exposed structure before foam placement.
Why it matters Direct foam contact with a vessel under suction risks catastrophic haemorrhage. If in doubt, escalate before proceeding.
📅 Dressing Change Frequency
✅ Standard wound — every 48–72 hrs
⚠ Infected wound — every 24 hrs
Note Frequency may be adjusted by the treating team based on wound aetiology, exudate level, and clinical response.
🔔 Troubleshooting Alarms
  1. Inspect all drape borders — lift and re-apply any lifted edges.
  2. Smooth out air bubbles trapped beneath the drape.
  3. Apply additional drape strips over any persistent gaps.
  4. Check the port pad is centred and firmly adherent over the access hole.
  5. If leak persists, replace the entire dressing.

  2. Trace the tubing for kinks or patient-positioning compression points.

  4. Check the inline filter — replace if visibly contaminated.
  5. Confirm the foam has not fully collapsed or been compressed by positioning.
